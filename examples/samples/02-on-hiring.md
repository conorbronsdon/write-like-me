I've hired about forty engineers. I got maybe six of those decisions badly wrong, and every one of them looked great on paper.

The pattern was boring. Strong résumé, smooth interview, no hard questions from me, because I liked them. Liking a candidate is not a signal. It's a bias with good manners.

So now I do one thing differently. I ask each candidate to walk me through something that broke and what they did the next morning. Not the outage. The next morning. People who learn from failure tell that part in detail. People who don't skip straight to the lesson, and the lesson always sounds like a LinkedIn post.

It isn't a perfect filter. Nothing is. But it's caught two people I would have hired, and one of them told me later he'd been fired from his last job for exactly the thing I was probing. He was relieved someone asked.

Ask about the next morning. It's the cheapest interview question you'll ever add.
